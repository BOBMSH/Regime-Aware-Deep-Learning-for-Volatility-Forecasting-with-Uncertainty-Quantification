The checklist's four Chapter 5 blocks

  • Interpretation — answer each RQ substantively; interpret rather than restate; compare explicitly with prior work; explain counterintuitive results by mechanism; avoid overclaiming.
  • Limitations — dataset, methodology and evaluation, “presented analytically, not as an apology.”
  • Implications — practical implications for the domain; ethics substantively, not in one sentence; what deployment would require.
  • Future work — specific and grounded in stated limitations. Split adopted: Ch5 names the direction each limitation implies in a clause; Ch6 prioritises them with resource considerations, which is what the 80+ band asks for.

The supervisor's brief, reduced to five operating rules

  • A numerical restatement is a result, not a discussion. Every claim needs why.
  • “Better architecture” and “good data” are not explanations. Explain by data distribution, model capacity, preprocessing, loss geometry, task structure, or the literature.
  • Organise around the research questions and objectives; for each, state what the results show, why, how it answers the question, what it means in the application context, and what remains uncertain.
  • Compare with literature only where metric, dataset and task are commensurable — and say so explicitly where they are not.
  • No “as can be seen”, no “performed well”, no “it is important to note”. Every exhibit is introduced, referenced and interpreted.

5.2 — Why the benchmark is hard to displace

RQ1 · objectives (i) and (ii)

Finding

Answer RQ1 in the negative, plainly: the networks lower pooled QLIKE by up to 5.4%, but no pairwise test detects it (DM p = 0.459) and the Model Confidence Set retains HAR-RV at 0.321. What the networks do displace is the conditional-variance family, excluded at 0.0005 and below.

Mechanism

The whole result is level, not timing. HAR-RV is fitted in levels by OLS, so it targets a conditional mean and, on a right-skewed target, sits far above the typical day — median forecast/actual 1.56 in calm. The networks estimate a conditional mean of log RV and retransform with a single global variance, landing near the median. QLIKE's asymmetry penalises under-prediction, so HAR's upward bias in calm is an asset under this loss, not an accident. This one sentence is the chapter's most valuable: it explains the calm-state loss, the flat interval and the rescale constant at once.

Evidence

Table 5.1 (mean and median forecast/actual by state). Mincer–Zarnowitz slopes 1.07 HAR against 1.25 for the mixture and 1.41 for the plain LSTM, R² 0.52–0.64 — all in the published range for daily RV, so no model is degenerate and the difference is one of calibration in level.

Literature

Corsi (2009) — HAR-RV's cascade is a strong prior, not a weak baseline. Hansen and Lunde (2005) — 330 GARCH specifications improved on none; our MCS excluding the whole conditional-variance family is the realised-volatility analogue and is stronger than their result, because here the family is beaten rather than merely tied. Christensen et al. (2023) report ML beating HAR on daily RV; say directly that our margin points the same way but does not reach significance, and give the reason (784 test days, one dominant episode).

Humility

Every loss is computed against a proxy (Table 3.5, row 1). A 5.4% difference on 784 days with one stress episode is not a basis for a general claim about architectures.

5.3 — Where regime conditioning pays, and why

RQ2 · objective (iii)

Finding

Regime conditioning relocates the advantage rather than raising it. Pooled, the three regime-conditioned specifications are separated from the unconditional network by less than their own seed spread. Conditionally, every network beats HAR-RV in the transitional state by 18–22%, the mixture furthest, and the Giacomini–White test says the identity of the better forecast is predictable at the forecast origin (χ² = 40.13, p = 9.99e-09). That is the dissertation's strongest claim and it should be named as such.

Mechanism

Two parts. (a) Transitional days are where the level assumption breaks: HAR's fixed cascade weights carry a calm-period level into a rising one, while the mixture's gate re-weights toward an expert fitted on that behaviour. (b) The state is a coarse, persistent signal — mean duration 15.1 days — so it can only help where the level is changing faster than the 22-day aggregate adapts. That predicts exactly the observed pattern: no help in calm, no help in crisis (where every model is already at a high level), help in between.

Evidence

Table 4.3's reversal; 65.0% of 329 transitional days favour the mixture (sign-test z = 5.46); the 10% trimmed mean moves only −0.0516 → −0.0466; two comparisons survive Holm at 6.44e-08. Note that both realised-volatility-only variants beat HAR-RV in calm (0.2829 and 0.2920 against 0.3008) while the three that also take the daily return fall behind — evidence that the calm-state deficit is the return feature, not the regime signal.

Literature

This is the Kılıç (2025) confrontation Chapter 2 promised. Kılıç finds regime-switching HAR beating deep models under QLIKE on the same target; we broadly confirm it pooled and qualify it conditionally — placing the regime signal inside the network yields no pooled gain but a state-predictable one. Blake et al. (2025) report material gains from soft Markov-switching regimes against an autoregressive baseline; our comparison is against a far stronger baseline, which is the likely source of the difference. Nystrup et al. (2020) — the jump penalty bought the persistence the conditioning needs, and λ = 1.5 would have changed little.

Humility

The crisis subsample is 76 days and its tests are weak (Table 3.5, row 4). The state count was fixed, not selected — and the two estimators' information criteria disagree, so no criterion endorses three.

5.4 — Why the intervals are too narrow and do not adapt

RQ3 · objective (iv)

Finding

Both clauses of RQ3 answer negative, and for the same reason. Coverage is 13.4–17.9% against a nominal 10; the independence test rejects nothing, so the bands are the wrong width, not the wrong shape; and predictive standard deviation in logs is flat at 0.5974 / 0.5965 / 0.6004 across the three states.

Mechanism

One mechanism, three symptoms. The retransformation uses a single global residual variance, so (i) the interval cannot widen in crisis, (ii) the calm state carries almost all the miscalibration — rescale constant 1.24 in calm against 1.01 and 1.02 elsewhere — and (iii) the same global variance is the level error of §5.2. Separately, the epistemic component supplies only 0.43% of predictive variance because a single recurrent layer leaves the dropout mask on the output layer alone, so the Monte Carlo passes perturb the head and nothing else. Say "answered no by construction" in those words — it is honest, and it converts a negative result into a design finding.

Evidence

Figure 5.1; Tables 4.4 and 4.5; the ×1.11 pooled rescale and the per-state constants; upper-tail violation 8.3% with dynamic-quantile p = 9.0e-06.

Literature

Discharge Chapter 3 §3.5's explicit promise — the only forward commitment Ch3 makes to Ch5 — that the discussion weighs Gal and Ghahramani's (2016) approximation against the under-coverage found. The verdict: the approximation is not wrong, it is inert at this depth; the diagnosis is architectural, not Bayesian. Ovadia et al. (2019) predict exactly this direction of failure under distribution shift. Song et al. (2025) and Dudek et al. (2025) put quantile intervals on realised volatility; our quantile head is the worst calibrated at 17.9%, which is worth stating against them rather than omitting.

Humility

Coverage is measured against the proxy, not the latent variance. Comparing a subsample against nominal would report the global level failure as a timing failure — do not do it; the comparison is always subsample against the rest.

5.5 — What transfers, and what the transfer test measured

RQ4 · no objective of its own

Finding

The conditional finding reproduces on the Russell 2000 (p = 0.0087) and not on the FTSE 100 (p = 0.254) — and the FTSE is the cleaner test.

Mechanism

Two candidate explanations, and the chapter should separate them rather than choose. (a) Measurement: the Russell's realised measure halves the squared session return (ratio 2.01 against 0.90 and 1.17), the non-synchronous-trading bias of a two-thousand-constituent small-cap index at five-minute sampling. (b) Session: the FTSE trades a different session, so the state model, re-estimated per asset, is labelling a different object — SPX splits 379/329/76 against FTSE 376/339/80 and RUT 228/474/86. A “transitional” day is not the same object across assets, which limits what a replication can mean.

Evidence

Table 4.6; the three Epps ratios; the per-asset state splits.

Literature

Liu, Patton and Sheppard (2015) document the sampling case directly.

Humility

Two assets, one frequency, overlapping windows. The honest formulation is direction replicates, magnitude does not — effect size out of market is roughly a third of the S&P 500's.

5.6 — Position against the closest prior work

The identified gap · 20% criterion

Job

Chapter 2 built Table 2.1 and claimed a gap of integration. Chapter 5 must now report what integrating actually bought — and it is not what the framing implied. Do not re-list the table; answer it.

The answer

Integration composes on the point layer and fails on the uncertainty layer. The combined model leads every interval criterion among the four probabilistic specifications yet is no better calibrated than any of them, so composing the stack improves relative ranking without fixing the absolute failure.

Neighbours

Isik and Yalcinkaya (2026) show the full stack composing outside finance; report that its financial instantiation composes on accuracy and not on calibration. Gunnarsson et al. (2024) named probabilistic uncertainty-aware ML as the open direction; we occupied it and found the direction harder than the framing suggests. Kim and Won (2018) and Bucci (2020) sit on different targets and horizons — say plainly that the comparison is not commensurable rather than quoting their numbers.

Contribution

State it in one sentence a marker can quote: the value of the regime signal on this target is conditional rather than average, and it is measurable ex ante. Chapter 2 pre-committed to reporting a null as readily as a headline — point at that sentence and show the commitment was kept.

5.7 — The confidence these findings can carry

“Appropriately evaluating the confidence that should be placed in any findings” · 10%

Job

This section exists because the grid names it. It is where the study's honesty is scored, and it should read as strength, not apology. Discharge Table 3.5's residual-risk column row by row rather than restating the table.

Content

The pooled ordering is not resolved by the evidence — across five initialisations the mixture's own standard deviation (0.0062) exceeds the published margin (0.0025) and the five-seed mean flips the ranking; reproducible is not robust. The conditional result is a different quantity, rests on 329 days at p < 1e-08, and is untouched. Add: the feature set and state-count grid were not pre-registered while the penalty grid was; the crisis subsample is small; per-regime tests get Holm; and the interpreter finding — an artefact rebuilt under a different interpreter version disagreed materially with the committed one, which is the concrete justification for the lock file and is reserved for this chapter.

Register

Qualified, specific, unhedged about what is established. “Not resolved by the evidence assembled here” beats “may not be entirely conclusive”.

5.8 — Implications for risk management, and the ethical position

“Demonstration of benefit of work undertaken” · 10%

Practical

Translate each finding into a decision. A desk gains nothing from swapping HAR-RV for a network on average loss — but a desk that knows the state at the origin can select between them, and that is implementable because the state is filtered, not smoothed. The interval result is the operationally serious one: a 90% band realising 86.6% and an upper tail realising 8.3% against 5% understates the loss side of a variance forecast, and that cost falls on whoever bears the exposure. Give the recipe and its limit — a single global scale of 1.11 restores nominal coverage, but the constant is 1.24 in calm and ~1.01 elsewhere, so one number cannot serve all states.

Deployment

Cheap to run (13.9 minutes, four cores, 390 MB, no accelerator); the binding constraint is the daily realised measure, and the library used here was discontinued in February 2022 — a deployed system needs a maintained intraday feed. Retraining cadence follows from the frozen-protocol check: the frozen fit costs about 1.6%, so refitting is worth roughly that much per period.

Ethics

Extend §3.10 rather than repeat it. No personal data, so individual fairness does not arise; the live asymmetry is that an over-tight interval transfers risk from the modeller to the exposure holder. Add two points Ch3 does not make: transparency — a mixture-of-experts gate is inspectable at the forecast origin, which matters where a model informs regulatory capital; and accountability — reporting the seed sensitivity is part of the ethical position, since a published ranking that a reseed reverses would mislead a user who cannot see it.

08Prose rules and the verification gate

House rules carried from Chapters 1–4

  • Terminology is fixed by Table 3.1 and Table C.2 — model names never vary. Realised variance throughout. Every conditional statistic buckets on the t−1 state.
  • QLIKE to four decimals; p-values in scientific notation below 0.001; percentages to one decimal. Name the window for any ratio.
  • Banned: “as can be seen”, “Table 5.1 shows the results”, “the model performed well”, “the results were satisfactory”, “it is important to note”, “tested empirically”.
  • Section numbers typed bare — the template auto-numbers. Captions carry keep-with-next; a figure caption sits below its figure and the picture paragraph carries the keep instead.
  • No superscripts and no arrows in cells; em dashes are fine. Chapter 5 introduces no new citations that Chapter 2 did not establish, except Liu, Patton and Sheppard (2015), already cited in Chapters 3 and 4.

Gate — run before declaring the chapter done

  • Each of RQ1–RQ4 has a substantiated answer, and RQ1's and RQ3's are stated as negative without softening.
  • Every claim of the form “X outperformed Y” is followed by a mechanism, not a restatement.
  • Each of the four objectives in §1.5 is visibly discharged.
  • Kılıç (2025), Blake et al. (2025), Gunnarsson et al. (2024), Isik and Yalcinkaya (2026), Christensen et al. (2023), Hansen and Lunde (2005) and Gal and Ghahramani (2016) each appear with a verdict — confirmed, contradicted or extended.
  • Where a comparison is not commensurable, the chapter says so instead of quoting the number.
  • Every row of Table 3.5's residual-risk column is answered somewhere in the chapter.
  • Ethics runs beyond a sentence and beyond “no personal data”.
  • Both exhibits are introduced in the text, captioned informatively, and interpreted.
  • No new experiment, no new result the reader has not already met in Chapter 4 — except Table 5.1's diagnostics, explicitly framed as analysis of the published forecasts.
  • Package verification as for Chapter 4: parts intact, only document.xml and its rels changed, pPr order clean, sectPr last, grids at 9026 twips, render and check every caption against its exhibit's page.

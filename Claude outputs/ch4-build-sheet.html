01

The rule that governs every paragraph

The brief states it in five words: results are facts; discussion is meaning. Chapter 4 says what happened. Chapter 5 says why. Most marks are lost by blending them — a results chapter that explains mechanisms leaves the discussion with nothing but repetition, and the grid penalises both.

Four moves for every table and figure. No exceptions.

  1. Introduce. What was evaluated, on what data, with which configuration — before the table appears.
  2. Present. An informative caption that says what the item contains, not "Table 4.1: Results".
  3. Interpret. State the pattern the reader should see and its magnitude. Never "as can be seen".
  4. Link. Name the research question or objective it bears on — then stop. The mechanism is Chapter 5's.

Lead with the uncomfortable result, do not bury it

The deep models' pooled edge over HAR-RV is not statistically significant (Diebold–Mariano p = 0.459), and the five-seed sweep shows the ordering among the leading models is inside initialisation noise. The instinct is to soften this. Do the opposite: state it in §4.1, and let §4.7 show where the models are separable. The grid's 80+ band asks for "conclusions fully justified and supported by the evidence presented" and for the candidate to "appropriately evaluate the confidence that should be placed in any findings" — that criterion is worth 10% and it is won by this decision alone. Chapter 2 §2.8 already pre-committed you to reporting this outcome as readily as a win. Honouring that pre-registration is the strongest single move available.

4.5

Regime identification

~260 w · Tables 4.4–4.5 · Figure 4.2
Why here
The regime signal is an input to everything in §4.7. Its validity has to be established before any result conditioned on it can be read.
Numbers
Selected penalty λ = 3.0 by the pre-registered rule; test-window mean state duration 15.1 days. The information criterion is reported as a sensitivity and disagrees with itself: the Baum-Welch HMM's BIC minimises at K = 3, the penalised model's at K = 4 — so "take the argmin" returns two state counts for one study, which is exactly why §3.3 fixes K in advance. The jump BIC rises monotonically 7,514 → 15,745 across λ = 1 → 300, so BIC would select λ = 1, giving 1,751 switches and a 3.16-day mean duration — the precise pathology the penalty exists to remove.
The strongest evidence you have, and it is currently unused
Fitted unsupervised on standardised daily returns alone, with no crisis labels anywhere in the estimator, the crisis state recovers 2001-09-17 (the 9/11 reopening), 2008-09-15 (Lehman, to the day), 2010-05-06 (the Flash Crash), 2016-06-24 (Brexit), 2018-02-05 (Volmageddon) and 2020-02-25 → 2020-05-08 (COVID). Spearman correlation with the VIX is 0.789; the calm state holds 1,741 low-VIX days against 106 high, the crisis state 0 low against 437 high. Table 4.5 lists the episodes; Figure 4.2 plots the state path against realised variance.

RQ2 — state dependence

~300 w · Table 4.7
Numbers
Bucketed on the t−1 state (379 calm / 329 transitional / 76 crisis). Regime-LSTM-B: 0.3172 / 0.1851 / 0.3211. HAR-RV: 0.3008 / 0.2367 / 0.3070. In the transitional state the gap is −0.0516, p = 4.3e-09; in calm, HAR-RV wins. The Giacomini–White joint test of B against HAR-RV on the three lagged state indicators: χ² = 39.83, p = 1.16e-08.
Robustness, in the same section
The transitional win is not outlier-driven: 65.0% of the 329 days favour B (sign test z = 5.46), the 10%-trimmed mean moves only −0.0516 → −0.0466, and the ten best days supply 29% of the difference — so 71% comes from the other 319. And the centring qualification §3.7 declared: the uncentred statistic is 37.91, p = 2.96e-08. Thirty tests, 0.12–4.87% shrinkage, no verdict changes.
Honesty
Report the multiplicity position plainly: the per-regime family is fifteen tests without correction. The headline survives any correction; two marginal results (p = 0.019, 0.022) do not. Saying so is worth more than the two results are.

RQ3 — interval quality

~300 w · Tables 4.8–4.9
Coverage
At a nominal 90%, PICP is 0.866 — a 13.4% violation rate against 10%. Kupiec rejects (p = 0.0025); Christoffersen's independence test does not (p = 0.514). Report that pair together: the failure is one of level, not clustering. The upper tail is worse — 8.3% against 5% expected, Kupiec p = 1.05e-04, dynamic quantile p = 8.8e-06 — which matters because it is the tail a risk manager is exposed to. Quantile-LSTM is worse again at 17.9%.
The measurement §3.7 promised
The scale constant that calibrates coverage is 1.11, and the CRPS-optimal scale is 1.18 on a plateau of 1.11–1.25. By state it is 1.24 in calm, 1.01 transitional, 1.02 crisis — the miscalibration is concentrated in the calm state, which is a sharper finding than the pooled number and points straight at Chapter 5's remedy.
Width, and the null result to state outright
Mean predictive spread on the log scale is flat at 0.5965–0.6004 across all three states, while interval width spans 5.1e-5 → 9.3e-4 — so the band is a fixed multiplicative collar, not an adaptive one. The epistemic share of predictive variance is 0.43%. RQ3's second clause, whether width adapts to regime, is answered no, and by construction: with one recurrent layer the dropout mask reaches the output layer only, exactly as §3.5 warned. Say it in those words — a predicted null result is a stronger finding than an unexplained one.
Also
Quantile-LSTM 0.3216 against Quantile-LSTM-mean 0.2742 confirms §3.5's median-versus-mean argument quantitatively; CRPS on the common three-point grid is 3.378e-5 against a closed form of 4.09e-5, which is why §3.7 refuses to compare them directly.

RQ4 — transfer

~200 w · Table 4.10
Numbers
.RUT: deep 0.2143 vs HAR-RV 0.2260, DM p = 0.153, GW p = 0.0087 — replicates (n = 788). .FTSE: 0.2905 vs 0.2997, DM p = 0.605, GW p = 0.254 — direction only (n = 795). Disclose

Realised variance should exceed the squared session return, not halve it — this is non-synchronous trading in a 2,000-constituent small-cap index at five-minute sampling (Liu, Patton and Sheppard 2015, already cited in Ch3). It is why GARCH and EGARCH over-predict .RUT by 1.9× and 1.7×. The HAR / RW / Regime-LSTM-B comparison is unaffected — all three read the same series — but .FTSE is the cleaner transfer test, and saying so is a mark of judgement rather than a caveat.

Robustness and sensitivities

~220 w · Table 4.11
Why it exists
This section is where the 80+ band is won. The grid asks for "exhaustive analysis", "outstanding technical competence" and for the candidate to evaluate "the confidence that should be placed in any findings". Six independent checks, in one table, each with its verdict.
The six
Seed sweep — five seeds over the four leading models; Regime-LSTM-B's standard deviation is 0.0062 against a published gap of 0.0025, and the five-seed mean reverses the ordering. Seed 17 reproduces the published fits to 5.6e-17, so this is initialisation variance, not a defect. Refit sensitivity — refitting at the baselines' monthly cadence gives LSTM 0.2607 against the frozen 0.2650, so the frozen protocol cost the network ~1.6% and the headline is a conservative lower bound, exactly as §3.6 predicted. Second regime estimator — the whole pipeline re-run on the maximum-likelihood comparator. Reproducibility gate — 943 published values re-derived by an independent implementation, 941 pass, 2 skipped, 0 mismatches.

The seed sweep is not a caveat attached to the result; it is the instrument that tells the reader how much of the pooled ordering to believe. Frame it that way.

05

House rules for the drafting

Terminology, fixed by Table 3.1 and Table C.2 — do not vary it

Econometric baseline · reference floor · unconditional network · regime-conditioned network · regime signal · ablation · sensitivity · headline and comparator profile. Model names are those of Table C.2 and change nowhere: LSTM-RVonly, not "the RV-only model". The brief names inconsistent terminology as a specific failure, and Chapter 3 has already paid for the fix.

Banned outright

"Table 4.1 shows the results" · "as can be seen" · "as presented in Figure 1" · "the model performed well" · "the results were satisfactory" · "it is important to note" · "tested empirically". Chapters 1–3 currently contain zero instances of these across all four documents. Keep the record.

Reporting conventions

  • QLIKE to four decimals, p-values in scientific notation below 0.001, percentages to one decimal.
  • Every conditional statistic buckets on the t−1 state. Say "lagged" once, then rely on it.
  • Realised variance throughout, never volatility — and give the annualised equivalent once, in §4.2, so the reader can convert.
  • Name the window whenever quoting a ratio: the overnight-gap excess is 1.89× on the test window, not the "~40%" figure that appears in a config comment.
  • Section numbers are typed bare — no hand-typed "4.1", the template auto-numbers Heading 1–4.

Reserved for Chapter 5 — write none of it here

The level-versus-timing mechanism (HAR-RV fitted in levels targets a conditional mean and sits above the typical calm day; the networks target a conditional mean of log RV and land near the median, and QLIKE's asymmetry punishes them for it — one mechanism explaining the calm-state loss, the flat interval and the 1.11 rescale together). The MSE/QLIKE disagreement on .RUT, where 88% of the squared error comes from twenty days. The confrontation with Kılıç (2025). The dropout-approximation weighing §3.5 explicitly promises Chapter 5 will do. And the reproducibility finding reserved verbatim in the project record. All of it is discussion.

06

Order of work

Roughly four working sessions. Draft in dependency order, not document order — the sections that fix vocabulary and units come first.

  1. Session 1 — the ground. §4.2, §4.4, §4.5, and the two figures. These fix the units, the configuration and the validity of the regime signal; every later section leans on them.
  2. Session 2 — the answers. §4.6, §4.7, §4.9. Three sections with the same shape: introduce, table, pattern, link to the question.
  3. Session 3 — the hard one. §4.8 and §4.10. The interval section carries three separate findings (level, tail, flat width) and needs the most careful sequencing; the robustness section needs its verdicts stated crisply.
  4. Session 4 — the frame. §4.1, §4.3, §4.11. Written last, once the findings are settled, so the introduction previews what is actually there and the worked example lands against known results.

That is a re-cut, not a rewrite — but it is cheaper to know first.
